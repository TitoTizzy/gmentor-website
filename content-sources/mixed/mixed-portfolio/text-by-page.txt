Greetings, I am Marie Gaëlle Mentor, an architect with a 
diverse portfolio that spans both local and international 
realms, primarily focused on residential constructions. 
I embarked on my educational journey with a 
Bachelor’s in Architecture from GOC University in 
Port-au-Prince, Haiti, laying the groundwork for a 
career marked by innovation and unwavering 
dedication. 
 
During the initial years, I played pivotal roles as an Architectural Designer and 
Site Supervisor with Gecim Construction in Haiti from February 1995 to August 
1997, specializing in crafting residential spaces within my homeland. Venturing 
beyond Haiti, I embraced challenges and opportunities on the global 
architectural stage, showcasing my adaptability and proficiency in residential 
design during my tenure as an Architectural Designer in the United States, 
particularly with AWA.

Townhouse in Waterbury, Connecticut. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The modern multi-story residential structure is characterized by its sleek design featuring clean lines and a minimalist aesthetic, 
typical of contemporary architecture. With a vertical orientation maximizing space efficiency, the building is clad in horizontal 
blue-grey vinyl siding, known for its durability and low maintenance. Symmetrically placed windows on each floor optimize 
natural light and ventilation, enhancing energy efficiency. Topped with a gable roof offering both visual appeal and practical 
benefits such as efficient water drainage and increased attic space, the structure is still undergoing construction, evident from 
the visible dirt and unfinished landscaping, capturing the ongoing evolution of architectural projects.

Beach House in Pierre Payen, Montrouis, Haiti. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
  
 
 
Perched on the edge of the shoreline in Pierre Payen, Montrouis, Haiti, this beach house embodies the epitome of coastal living. 
Despite the challenges posed by the location, which included navigating civil unrest, the difficulties were not technical but 
logistical, making the project both thrilling and demanding. We worked tirelessly, day and night, to overcome obstacles and

months, this beach house stands as a testament to resilience, showcasing the seamless blend of functional elegance and relaxed

vibrant backdrop of Haiti's coastal beauty.

Extension of the Unite Centrale Execution (UCE) office. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
  
 
The project does not involve a high degree of technical complexity. It consists of constructing a lightweight structure for the 
extension of the Central Extension Unit (UCE) office using means and methods that allow for a rapid demolition of the built 
structure in case there is a need to restore the site (e.g., relocation). The difficulty primarily lay in considering, during the design 
phase, the constraints of the project site, notably: difficult site access; the preservation of the existing irregularly shaped pool; 
and the protection of existing trees and shrubs around the pool. Significantly, this construction was executed directly on the 
location of the existing pool, requiring careful planning and execution to integrate the new structure seamlessly with the unique 
features of the site.
